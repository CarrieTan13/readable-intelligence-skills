# Verification and delivery

This file supports Stages 5–7 of the workflow in `SKILL.md`. The preservation requirements §§P1–P4 in `SKILL.md` take precedence over everything here.

## V1. Checks

Complete the structural and prose revision before judging the outcome. Compare the entire original and revised manuscripts, paying particular attention to all changed and relocated passages and their surrounding context. Use the following checks and record their actual scope and status. The Stage column gives the stage in which each check is run; a check run in Stage 5 is run again for every unit changed later, including changes made after a review round.

| Check | Stage | Required assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage and complete source | 5, 7 | Every section, appendix, statement, and proof is accounted for; the delivered LaTeX contains the complete editable manuscript, with no omitted arguments or PDF-page substitutes. |
| Mathematical preservation | 5 | Compare all formal statements and displays, and every changed or relocated proof passage, with the authoritative source. Check prose hypotheses, quantifiers, constants, parameter order, and distinct cases as well as symbols. Source comparisons must retain mathematical font and decoration commands, including `\mathcal`, `\mathfrak`, `\mathbf`, `\boldsymbol`, and `\mathsf`, and check the macro definitions that determine them; normalization must not erase these distinctions. Screen changed passages for deleted or altered qualifications and verify each flag in context. Keyword counts are warning signals, not a passing criterion: unchanged counts do not establish unchanged scope or logical direction. |
| Dependencies and notation | 5 | Reordering preserves prerequisites and scope; references resolve correctly; symbols are understandable at their use; no unsupported claim or new formal summary statement has appeared. |
| Notation refinements | 5 | Each renaming or abbreviation substitution has been checked at all affected uses, including formal statements and displays; binding, scope, types, conditioning, dependencies, and meaning are preserved; no variable capture or symbol collision is introduced. |
| Labels and citations | 5 | All original visible results and citations remain accounted for; any relabeling is consistent; bibliography entries and deferred-proof pointers are correct; every later citation of a split statement points to the part it uses. In the rendered PDF, check the destination of each displayed number in grouped references, not merely the existence of destinations. Check that reference types are not separated from their numbers and that external locators remain external. |
| Literature paraphrases | 5 | Substantive descriptions of prior results have the source comparisons required by §S5, including those outside the proof. Missing evidence and suspected discrepancies are recorded separately; citation existence alone is not a pass. |
| External applications | 5 | Important parameter substitutions and applicability conditions are clear; unresolved checks are reported honestly; routine uses have not acquired repetitive audits. |
| Duplication and editorial residue | 5 | Every deduplicated mathematical passage has the retained destination and same-setting comparison required by §P2; no distinct assertion, case, inference, or qualification was lost. No new redundant roadmap, repeated application discussion, identity-substitution proof, placeholder, or editorial provenance remains in the manuscript. Check that proof-stage headings govern the intended content and that phrases such as "The claim follows" still have an identifiable referent after rewriting. Inspect context rather than treating every keyword match as a defect. |
| Build and layout | 5, 7 | Compile when tools are available and inspect the rendered output. Check changed pages, affected floats, contents, numbering, whitespace, URL breaks, and proof continuity, including run-in proof-stage headings and an opening "Proof." that is not stranded on its own line. Compare document fonts, theorem-heading styles, and table rules with the original; every departure needs the located repair reason required by §N1. Preserve readable text and vector figures. |
| Reader benefit | 6 | For each consequential edit, identify what becomes easier to understand or locate. Check for new verbosity, premature notation, mechanical transitions, and loss of useful original context. |
| Numbering and relabeling | 5 | Formal statements, numbered displays, and sections follow one consecutive numbering scheme without letter suffixes, primes, or "bis" numbers. The relabeling map covers every changed identifier, and every reference resolves to the intended item, including references to split statements. |
| Bibliography integrity | 5 | Every bibliography entry is cited in the delivered manuscript and every citation resolves to an entry. Removed entries are recorded with reasons. No access notes, duplicate identifiers, or format inconsistencies remain. |
| Inventories | 5 | The definition inventory shows one definition per object and one statement per convention, with any reminders marked. The coined-term inventory has a decision for every term and recounts in the revision, with no unexplained increase. Every pervasive pattern has counts by class before and after revision, and no occurrence of a class to be changed remains unrecorded. |
| Relocated commentary | 5 | Every passage moved out of a formal statement has a destination allowed by §P1 and reads as mathematical prose rather than as instructions. |
| Editorial checklist | 5 | Every item of the editorial checklist has been applied to the whole revised manuscript, including inherited material, and every Stage 2 finding has a disposition. |
| Independent review | 6, 7 | The designated reviewer completed the required rounds and batches under `agents/checklist-reviewer.md` with the stated configuration; the batches jointly covered the manuscript; every finding has a disposition; no blocking finding or failed pervasive fix remains open without a documented reason. A self-review does not satisfy this check. |


Report source-preservation checks, checks of the written argument, formal proof checks, and checks of correspondence between the formalization and manuscript separately. For the written argument, inspect inference transitions, the scope of assumptions, coverage of cases, and whether invoked results support the stated conclusions, especially in changed or relocated passages. Record the passages examined and unresolved questions. Agreement with the baseline establishes fidelity only; successful formal proof checking does not certify every step of the accompanying written exposition. This editorial workflow does not require reproving the entire manuscript or running unavailable formalizations. Do not describe unexamined portions as verified or infer whole-proof correctness from local checks.

Hashes and source diffs can detect changes, but they do not certify preservation of meaning or proof validity. For reconstructed TeX, verify the original-to-transcription and transcription-to-revision steps separately; agreement between derived files cannot pass an unchecked original-to-transcription step. Do not report unavailable source-level checks as passed.

Inspect length changes by section to find repetition or misplaced expansion. Do not infer quality from a shorter manuscript, a larger edit count, successful compilation, or high textual similarity.

Attempt the applicable checks, correct failures within the preservation boundary, and rerun affected checks. Record unavailable tools, missing essential evidence, and unresolved failures. Complete the independent parts of the work without inventing a repair or claiming that all checks passed.

## V2. Reader comparison and restoration

Assess whether the intended reader can reconstruct the obstacle, central mechanism, main dependencies, and significance from the introduction without first consulting later definitions. Point to the passages that make this possible and record remaining obstacles. Check that simplification has not erased conditions, hidden a proof stage, or duplicated the technical account. This is an editorial assessment, not evidence of independent reader testing unless such testing occurred.

For supplied feedback, check each acceptance criterion against the delivered version. Confirm that the cited passage actually answers the recorded question and that scope, constructivity, novelty, consequences, and verification claims have the stated support. Reopen any disposition affected by later edits. An overall readability improvement does not establish that every individual concern has been addressed.

For each substantive revision, compare the original and revised local arrangements directly. If the original is better, restore the smallest coherent unit that recovers its advantage, including dependent neighboring material when necessary. Tie rule: on a tie, keep the revision when the change addresses a checklist finding or a pervasive pattern under §N5, because the checklist identifies the original form as a defect; restoring the original for such a change requires a located reason why the original reads better, recorded in the ledger or the disposition table. For other changes, retain the original on a tie unless a successful nearby change requires the revised arrangement. Preserve effective improvements elsewhere. Rebuild and recheck the areas affected by a restoration.

Independent review is required and follows `agents/checklist-reviewer.md`. Give the reviewer the original and revised manuscripts before the editor's justification. Distinguish independent review from self-review, report the actual reviewer and comparison scope, and resolve concrete original-better findings. Do not claim an independent comparison occurred when it did not.

## V3. Deliverables and editorial record

Deliver:

1. The complete revised LaTeX project, including bibliography and necessary figures, plus the built PDF when a build is available.
2. A concise change report explaining the consequential editorial improvements, their origins, important moves or relabelings, rejected suggestions when material, verification outcomes, and unresolved mathematical or transcription questions.
3. The revision ledger, statement/proof inventory, relabeling map, inventories, and verification record described in §W4, sufficient to reproduce and assess the work. They may be combined to avoid a collection of redundant reports.
4. The independent checklist review record of `agents/checklist-reviewer.md`: the reviewer configuration actually used, every round's and batch's report, and the disposition table, or the recorded reason why the designated reviewer could not be invoked together with the labeled self-review.

The section review record must cover every major section and distinguish its diagnosed difficulty, action, source location, revised location, and reader benefit. Retained sections need a specific rationale. The statement inventory must account for every formal statement and proof, including their original and revised locations and dispositions. Record significant dependency evidence, terminology changes, consequential notation refinements with their affected scope, and relabelings where applicable. Track incomplete stages by section when work is batched.

When community feedback was supplied, include the §R4 mapping in the ledger or change report. For each addressed item, provide a concise explanation of the response and an exact destination in the delivered manuscript, sufficient to support a later follow-up. Include partial and unresolved items with the evidence or action still needed. Keep commenter details and editorial tracking out of the manuscript itself.

Record input hashes, build and check commands, relevant environment details, output paths, failures, and the distinction between checks against the authoritative source and checks against reconstructed material. Keep full logs separate from the concise report when needed.

Do not replace the revised manuscript with an audit, a list of recommendations, or isolated edited excerpts. All artifacts must stay within the assigned workspace. Manuscript revision is complete only when the full review has been carried out, the consequential identified difficulties have been addressed or specifically documented as unresolved, the independent checklist review has been completed or its unavailability reported, the deliverables are complete, and the verification status is honestly reported.
