description: Restructure and rewrite a proof-based mathematics or theoretical computer science paper for substantially better understanding and navigation while preserving its mathematical content and complete arguments. Runs autonomously from the supplied manuscript, without approval requests. Use whenever a paper must be reorganized or rewritten for readability, including inherited problems such as bundled or overlong formal statements, commentary inside statements, defensive or procedural prose, coined jargon, redundant qualifiers, repeated definitions, and nonstandard front matter, numbering, bibliography, or cross-references. Applies a self-contained editorial checklist and an independent checklist review by a designated reviewer subagent.

# Restructure Paper

This skill consists of this file and six supporting files. Section identifiers are global: a reference such as §P1 or §N3 names a section in the file listed below, and A1–I6 name items of the editorial checklist.

The reviewer receives only `references/editorial-checklist.md` and the reviewer instructions in `agents/checklist-reviewer.md`; the checklist is therefore written to be applicable without the other files.

## W1. Purpose, scope, and inputs

This workflow governs a single assigned mathematics or theoretical computer science paper supplied as LaTeX or PDF. The objective is to make its argument substantially easier for its intended research audience to understand, follow, and check. Review the whole manuscript and address its important expository difficulties, including difficulties that require reorganizing a section or rewriting a sustained explanation.

Mathematical preservation is a hard constraint. Editorial success is judged by the reader's understanding of the argument. Neither the number of edits nor the amount of unchanged text is an objective. A few terminology fixes and cross-references do not complete the task when important structural difficulties remain. Conversely, do not rewrite effective passages simply to make every section look newly edited.

Determine the baseline under §W2. Use only the inputs supplied with the task and material that the environment makes available under §W2. Preserve raw sources and revision instructions. Prefer original LaTeX corresponding to the baseline when it is supplied, and check its rendering against the authoritative PDF. Confirm that the inputs represent the same version, and resolve discrepancies under §W2. When additional versions or review comments are explicitly supplied, evaluate their suggestions against the baseline: retain successful explanations, correct regressions, and preserve the main proof route. Do not access another revision workspace to discover or import material. The governing access policy takes precedence over this workflow.

If only a PDF is supplied, reconstruct a complete, editable LaTeX transcription inside the assigned folder. Preserve the PDF as the authoritative original, and keep the reconstructed baseline distinguishable from the edited manuscript. The delivered source must contain the complete text, statements, displayed mathematics, proofs, bibliography, and necessary figure assets. A source package consisting of original PDF pages plus replacement overlays does not satisfy this requirement. Preserve existing vector figure quality whenever possible.

Record transcription uncertainty. For PDF-only input, verify mathematical fonts and decorations against the original page images; extracted plain text cannot establish their identity. Distinguish comparison with the original PDF from comparison with reconstructed TeX. Do not claim verification against unavailable original labels, citation keys, or math-mode tokens.

Once a manuscript revision is assigned, run the workflow to completion without requesting approval, authorization, confirmation, or a choice from the user. Every decision is made by the rules of this skill, with the defaults in §W2 where the rules would otherwise leave a choice open. Missing external references and uncertain mathematics do not prevent independent editorial work. Preserve affected content and record the limitation separately. This workflow grants no filesystem or network permission beyond what the environment already provides. Editing these instruction files does not itself assign a manuscript revision.

## W2. Autonomous operation and default decisions

The workflow never pauses for the user. It makes each decision by the rules of this skill and records the decision and its reason in the revision ledger. When a rule would otherwise leave a choice open, apply the default below. When rules conflict, apply this precedence: the preservation requirements (§§P1–P4); honest reporting of verification status; the editorial checklist; the remaining guidance. Record a conflict resolved by precedence as a limitation in the change report.

| Situation | Automatic decision |
|---|---|
| Several versions of the manuscript are supplied | The baseline is the version that the task identifies as the manuscript to revise or as the original. Otherwise it is the version not marked as revised, rewritten, or edited; otherwise the earliest by the date printed in the document, then by file metadata. Every other version is an additional version under §W1. |
| LaTeX and PDF of the baseline disagree | The PDF is authoritative. Use the LaTeX as the working source, correct it to the PDF wherever their text or mathematics differ, and record each discrepancy. |
| No manuscript, or no readable manuscript, is supplied | Do not ask for one. Deliver a short report naming the missing or unreadable input and what could not be done. |
| A tool needed for a check is unavailable | Record the check as unavailable, continue the independent work, and never mark that check passed. |
| Network access | Never required. When the environment provides it, it may be used only to retrieve openly available copies of works the manuscript cites, for checking paraphrases, applications, and bibliographic data. Never post, contact anyone, or fetch material from another revision workspace. Without it, record the missing check. |
| Suspected mathematical error, gap, circularity, or ambiguity | Retain the content exactly, record its location and the evidence inspected, and report it. Never repair, reinterpret, or strengthen it. |
| A paraphrase of a cited result looks inaccurate | Retain it unchanged and report the discrepancy with its evidence. |
| Change of mathematical font or decoration | Only to correct a transcription error demonstrated by the authoritative PDF or original source, or to eliminate a notation alias that the manuscript itself declares (§P1). Otherwise never. |
| Adding a bibliography entry | Only when the work's bibliographic data and the specific cited statement have been verified in an inspected copy (§P3). Otherwise add nothing and record the issue. |
| Replacing a proof by a citation | Never. A proof that is disproportionate to its role moves to an appendix with every step retained (§S6). |
| A standard fact is cited to a tangential source | Cite instead a standard source already in the bibliography whose statement has been checked; otherwise keep the citation and record the issue (§N4). |
| A bibliography entry is no longer cited | Remove it and record the removal (§P3). |
| Classification codes and keywords | Keep existing ones. When they are absent, add Mathematics Subject Classification 2020 codes (one primary, any secondary) and a few keywords determined from the main theorem and principal methods, and record the choice (§N4). |
| The manuscript mixes cross-reference styles | Use bare parenthesized numbers for displays, as in "(3.2)", and the type with its number for results, as in "Lemma 3.2" (§N4). |
| Numbering after splitting or promoting statements | Ordinary consecutive numbering in the document's existing scheme, with a relabeling map (§P3). |
| A coined term has no clear replacement | Apply the decision rules of §N3; if they leave the term, keep it, define it at first use, and record the reason. |
| A reader-comparison verdict is a tie | Apply the tie rule of §V2. |
| A planned change still fails its preservation check after the repair permitted by correction loop 1 (§W4) | Restore the original for that unit and record the limitation. |
| The designated reviewer cannot be invoked | Record the failure and its cause, run a self-review that follows the same protocol and is labeled as such, report that independent review did not occur, and continue. |
| Supplied feedback has an unclear target | Treat it as general feedback (§R4). |

## W3. Execution sequence

Execute the following stages in order. The sections listed in the file map supply the detailed rules used at each stage. Complete the whole-manuscript diagnosis and revision plan before undertaking substantive rewriting. For long papers, batch implementation and verification by section while retaining the overall plan. No stage waits for approval.

| Stage | Read first | Actions | Output and completion condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Establish the baseline | This file; §R4 and §R5 when feedback or a style example is supplied | Identify the manuscript, the baseline (§W2), the supplied inputs, the access boundaries of the environment, and the deliverables. Read the entire manuscript. Preserve and hash the source. Prepare a separate working source; reconstruct complete editable LaTeX for PDF input under §W1. Attempt a baseline build and check transcription against the PDF. | Complete working source with a recorded baseline build status, input hashes, and located transcription uncertainties. If a tool is unavailable, record the limitation and continue independent work; do not mark the build or transcription check passed. |
| 2. Diagnose | `references/diagnosis-and-planning.md`, `references/editorial-checklist.md`, §N3 and §N5 in full; skim the rest of `references/restructuring.md` and `references/prose-and-notation.md` | Apply §R1–§R2 and assess the concerns in §§S1–S6 and §§N1–N5 for every section and appendix. Apply every item of the editorial checklist to the front matter, every section and appendix, every formal statement, and the bibliography, including problems inherited from the baseline. Build the definition, coined-term, and pattern inventories (§R3). Check substantive paraphrases of cited results under §S5. Inventory all formal statements and proofs. Identify reader difficulties, important dependencies, notation problems, repetition, and effective passages to retain. Examine nested prerequisite lookups, changes of representation, dense groups of parameters, and local estimate chains even when definitions, references, and headings already exist. | Section coverage and statement/proof inventory are complete. Every section has specific findings or an evidence-based retention rationale. Every checklist item has located findings with checklist IDs or a recorded `clear` or `not applicable` verdict stating the scope examined. The three inventories cover the whole manuscript. A section marked only “reviewed” does not satisfy this stage. |
| 3. Plan the revision | `references/restructuring.md`, `references/prose-and-notation.md` | Group findings into coherent changes. Checklist findings enter the plan like any other diagnosed difficulty. Plan each pervasive pattern under §N5 as one change covering every occurrence, decide every coined term under §N3, and decide every repeated definition under §R3. Set the proposed section order and proof stages, identify prerequisite moves, and select notation refinements. Assess where the existing deduction of the main theorem belongs, distinguish the principal argument from supporting verification, and group technical detail by its role. Move or replace unnecessary repetition before adding explanations; retain useful local reminders. For each planned change, specify the source location, proposed action, reader benefit, preservation risk, and check. Prioritize consequential improvements before local polishing. | An ordered plan in the revision ledger accounts for every important diagnosed difficulty, every checklist finding, every pervasive pattern, and every coined term. Record a proposed treatment, a justified decision to retain the original, or a specific unresolved limitation. Do not replace this plan with a list of generic writing advice. |
| 4. Implement | `references/restructuring.md`, `references/prose-and-notation.md` | Follow §§P1–P4, §§S1–S6, and §§N1–N5, in this order: (a) structural moves, including splits, promotions, relocation of commentary, and moves to appendices; (b) proof explanations and consequential external-application clarifications; (c) terminology replacements, elimination of declared aliases, consolidation of repeated definitions, and other notation refinements; (d) pervasive-pattern changes, applied to every occurrence of the classes to be changed (§N5); (e) sentence-level edits; (f) renumbering under §P3 once every statement is in its final position, then updating every reference. Work in coherent units; update dependent references and all occurrences of changed notation and terminology. Maintain the complete source throughout. | Each planned change has an implemented location or a recorded reason for rejection or deferral. All original statements and arguments remain accounted for. Update the ledger, inventories, and relabeling map as work occurs, rather than reconstructing them at the end. |
| 5. Verify preservation | `references/verification-and-delivery.md` (§V1), `references/editorial-checklist.md` | Run the checks that §V1 assigns to Stage 5, including the numbering, bibliography-integrity, and inventory checks, recount every pervasive pattern (§N5), recheck every changed paraphrase of a cited result (§S5), and re-apply the editorial checklist to the revised manuscript. Compare formal statements and displays against the authoritative baseline; check every changed or relocated proof passage, notation substitution, dependency, citation, and reference. Build and inspect affected output when tools are available. | Each implemented change has a recorded check result. Correct failures before treating the change as verified. Log unavailable checks and unresolved issues explicitly; compilation alone does not complete this stage. |

(a) Compare the entire original and revised manuscripts under §V2, examining changed passages with their surrounding context, and inspect rendered navigation, contents, page breaks, and affected figures. (b) Make the restorations that §V2 requires and send them through Stage 5. (c) Run review round 1 under `agents/checklist-reviewer.md` on the resulting manuscript. A stage's output is evidence of the work performed, not an invitation to insert corresponding summaries or formal statements into the manuscript. The sequence prescribes analysis, decisions, implementation, and verification; it does not require the same additions in every paper.

## W4. Working records and correction loops

Use the following compact supporting records, reusing equivalent existing files where appropriate:

- `revision-ledger.md`: the stage status, section coverage, ordered revision plan, and issue-level decisions. For each issue record an ID, original location, diagnosis and origin, proposed action, intended reader benefit, dependencies or preservation risks, revised location, current status, and verification or comparison result. A retained passage needs its reason; an unresolved issue needs its exact limitation and the next action or evidence required.
- `statement-inventory.csv`: one entry per original formal statement, with its original identifier and location, proof location where applicable, revised identifier and location, disposition, and preservation-check status. Account separately for standalone proofs or arguments not attached to a formal statement so they are not lost. An equivalent structured format is acceptable.
- `verification.log`: commands, relevant environment details, outputs or paths to full logs, and results of source, mathematical-preservation, reference, build, and layout checks. Distinguish passed, failed, and unavailable checks.
- `relabeling-map.csv`: every changed identifier of a formal statement, numbered display, section, or label, with its old and new form and the reason (split, promotion, renumbering, or relabeling), so that every later reference can be checked (§P3).
- `inventories/`: the definition inventory, coined-term inventory, and pattern inventory described in §R3, each with its baseline entries, decisions, and counts in the revision.
- `checklist-review/`: the reports of every independent review round and batch (`agents/checklist-reviewer.md`), the reviewer configuration actually used (model, reasoning effort, date, and the inputs supplied), and a disposition table. For each finding the table gives its ID, checklist item, location, severity, origin (inherited, introduced, or worsened), disposition (`fixed`, `rejected`, or `unresolved`), revised location or reason, and re-check result. For a pervasive finding, the table also gives the pattern-inventory counts by class before and after revision (§N5). Checklist findings made by the editor in Stage 2 enter the revision ledger with their checklist IDs.

These records may be combined when their required contents remain easy to locate.

Use issue statuses such as `open`, `planned`, `implemented`, `verified`, `retained`, and `unresolved`. `Implemented` means an edit exists; `verified` also requires its preservation checks and reader comparison to have succeeded. A retained or unresolved disposition must include a reason, not merely close a row. At delivery, reconcile all open work and report any unverified or incomplete work explicitly.

Apply these correction loops:

1. If a preservation, dependency, or notation check fails, return the affected unit to Stage 4. Repair it within §§P1–P4 or restore the original, then repeat Stage 5 for that unit and its affected uses.
2. If reader comparison finds a regression, or a tie for a change that the tie rule of §V2 does not protect, restore or revise the smallest coherent unit under §V2. Repeat its preservation checks and local reader comparison before closing the issue.
3. If a correction changes a definition's presentation, shared notation, ordering, or references, reopen checks for every dependent passage it affects. Unaffected successful checks need not be rerun.
4. If revision reveals a new substantial difficulty, add it to the ledger and revisit the relevant diagnosis and plan. Do not use it as a reason to abandon unfinished parts of the original plan.
5. If a problem cannot be resolved within the preservation or access boundary, retain the affected mathematical content, record the limitation as unresolved, and complete independent work. Never substitute a guessed repair or a false passing status.
6. If the independent reviewer reports a finding, treat it as a diagnosed difficulty: plan and implement a fix within §§P1–P4, or record a specific reason for rejection or an exact limitation, and send every fix through Stage 5 and a local §V2 comparison before the next review round. Inheritance from the baseline is not a reason for rejection.
7. If the reviewer reports a pervasive fix as failed, repeat §N5 for that pattern over the whole manuscript, not only at the reported locations, and update the pattern inventory before the next round.

Before delivery, confirm both that the manuscript is complete and that the important diagnosed reader difficulties have actually been addressed or specifically reported as unresolved. Completed paperwork, edit counts, and successful compilation alone do not establish editorial success.

## Preservation requirements

### P1. Formal statements and mathematical content

Preserve every definition, theorem, lemma, proposition, corollary, claim, conjecture, and distinct displayed formula. Preserve the mathematical content of repeated occurrences; the narrow deduplication rule in §P2 permits replacing a genuinely repeated exposition by a reference to its retained treatment. Preserve their hypotheses, quantifiers and quantifier order, conclusions, constants, parameter dependencies, and scope, including mathematics expressed in ordinary language.

Treat mathematical fonts and decorations as part of symbol identity throughout the manuscript, including inline mathematics, proofs, figures, tables, and captions. Preserve these distinctions. Routine notation refinement and equivalent typographic correction do not permit changing them. Change a font or decoration only to correct a transcription error demonstrated by the authoritative PDF or original source, or to eliminate a declared alias as described next. Record the scope and check every affected use.

When the manuscript itself declares two notations for one object, for example by writing that one symbol also denotes, equals, or abbreviates another, the two are a single object and the alias may be eliminated. Keep the notation used in the defining statement; if both are introduced together, keep the one with more occurrences. Replace every occurrence of the other, including formal statements, displays, figures, and captions, delete the declaration, and record the scope. Notations that the manuscript does not declare identical remain distinct, even when they appear to denote the same object.

Permitted changes within formal statements are non-mathematical grammatical corrections, equivalent typographic corrections subject to the preceding restriction, consistent relabeling, the notation refinements described below, equivalent rephrasing of mathematical content, relocation of commentary as described in the next paragraph, and splitting under §P3. Record these changes; for an equivalent rephrasing, record the clause-by-clause correspondence in the statement inventory.

Within a formal statement, distinguish mathematical content from commentary. Mathematical content comprises hypotheses, quantifiers and their order, conclusions, constants, parameter dependencies, and conditions on the order in which parameters are chosen; it stays in the statement and may be rephrased only equivalently. Commentary comprises usage advice, motivation, comparisons with other results, remarks on what the statement does not assert, and justifications of the statement's own claims. Relocate commentary in this order of preference: to the point of use when it concerns a specific later application; to a numbered or unnumbered Remark immediately after the statement when it concerns several uses; to the proof when it is a justification, which must then remain in the proof. Remove commentary that serves no reader need under §N1. Do not leave relocated commentary as a free paragraph between formal statements or appended to an unrelated proof. Relocated text must itself satisfy §N1 and the editorial checklist: rewrite instruction lists, accounting metaphors, and defensive phrasing as it moves; moving a passage unchanged does not address its prose. Classify each sentence before moving it: a sentence that restricts the order of choices or the dependence of constants is mathematical even when it is phrased as advice. An informal conclusion, such as a claim that a step "costs" a certain quantity or is "harmless", may be replaced by a precise assertion only when the supplied proof establishes that precise form; otherwise retain it and record it as unresolved.

A standing hypothesis that a statement uses only implicitly, for example a contradiction hypothesis fixed for an entire part of the paper, may be made explicit in the statement by citing its labeled display as a hypothesis ("Assume (H)."). Do not drop such a hypothesis because the proof appears not to use it; record that observation as a question about scope.

Consistent symbol renaming and exact substitution of an explicitly defined abbreviation are permitted in prose, formal statements, and displayed mathematics when they preserve mathematical meaning and improve readability. Apply the scope and substitution checks in §N2. These refinements do not permit changing a definition's substance, merging distinct objects, deleting a formal statement, or resolving an ambiguity by assumption. Do not resolve a genuinely ambiguous statement by choosing a stronger or different interpretation. Explain its intended role outside the statement only when that explanation is supported by the supplied argument.

### P2. Proofs and the proof route

Retain every necessary inference, intermediate calculation, distinct case, justification, and scientific qualification. Preserve the force and scope of restrictions, ordering conditions, negations, and sufficiency or necessity claims, including phrases such as "only," "before," "without," "not," and "suffices." Rephrasing must not weaken a qualification or turn a sufficient condition into a necessary one, or conversely. Long reasoning is not redundant merely because it is long. Preserve the main proof strategy and techniques.

Proofs may be subdivided into their existing stages, and independent supporting material may be reordered. Explanatory prose may be rewritten substantially. When moving a proof or construction, move the prerequisite definitions and parameter choices needed to understand it, or provide a precise existing reference. Do not introduce a new dependency or silently repair a suspected gap.

A repeated explanation or derivation may be replaced by a precise reference to its complete retained treatment only after checking that the assertion, objects, hypotheses, normalization, parameter dependencies, and order of choices are the same. Keep a short reminder when it helps the current argument. Retain every separately numbered formal statement, distinct formula, necessary inference, distinct case, and additional qualification. Similar calculations in different settings are not duplicates without this check. Record the removed occurrence, the retained destination, any local explanation kept, and the affected uses in the ledger. This permits eliminating duplicate exposition; it does not permit replacing a distinct proof by an external citation, narrowing a theorem, or omitting an argument because it appears standard.

A word count, math-token quota, or high text similarity does not establish proof completeness. Compare the actual arguments and their mathematical content.

### P3. Statement set, numbering, terminology, labels, and citations

Preserve the original set of mathematical assertions; do not add summary propositions or merge statements. Explain the role of a component in prose or point to an existing result that states its properties. Proof-stage headings are allowed without creating new mathematical claims.

A formal statement that bundles several independently usable results may be split into separate statements when later arguments cite its parts separately or when the bundle prevents readers from identifying what is asserted. Each part keeps its own hypotheses, quantifiers, constants, parameter dependencies, and order conditions, and no part loses a definition or conclusion that it needs. Do not split the cases of a single result, or separate a conclusion from its immediate quantitative refinement. Record the one-to-many mapping in the statement inventory and redirect every later citation of the original statement to the part it actually uses; a reference that still compiles is not evidence that it points to the right part.

Give each part of a split statement, and each lemma promoted from a proof, an ordinary number in the document's existing numbering sequence; renumber the following items and update every reference. Do not use letter suffixes, primes, or "bis" numbering, and convert any such numbering inherited from the baseline. Record every old and new identifier in the relabeling map. Do not create a part whose content is a one- or two-line consequence used only together with another part: keep it as an item or sentence of the statement it serves, or as a sentence of an adjacent remark. After splitting, give each part a title that describes that part's own content, or no title; a title inherited from the bundle usually fits only one part.

An intermediate assertion that a supplied proof already states and proves, and that other arguments cite, may be stated as a lemma together with its existing proof. This is a relocation, not a new result: record it in the inventory as derived from the parent proof, and leave the parent proof complete. A statement whose meaning depends on surrounding prose ("the data above", "the construction just given") may be made self-contained by naming the objects, definitions, and standing assumptions it uses; the prose definitions themselves remain.

Retain established technical terminology when its meaning is clear. Terms coined within the manuscript are not protected by inheritance: when a coined term, informal label, or metaphor obstructs reading, replace it with standard terminology or a short descriptive phrase, or define it once at first use and use it consistently (§N3). Refine notation under §N2 when doing so improves readability. Do not replace one mathematical notion with another without establishing equivalence from the supplied material. Record any permitted terminology change and its scope, and check every occurrence, including section and statement titles. Scoped reuse of a symbol is acceptable when the scopes are clear; global renaming of symbols is not a default remedy. Replacing a coined term follows §N3 and is a terminology change, not a symbol renaming.

Preserve every citation that remains in the text and the bibliographic identity of every cited work. When a sentence removed under an editorial rule contained the only citation of an entry, remove the entry as well. The delivered bibliography lists exactly the works cited in the delivered manuscript; record every removed entry with its reason. Correcting the presentation of an entry, such as removing access notes, duplicate identifiers, or format inconsistencies, is permitted when the cited work and version are unchanged (§N4). Preserve existing labels or record a complete relabeling map and update all uses. For PDF input, record the mapping from visible original numbers to reconstructed labels. Do not claim that reconstructed keys are the original source keys. In grouped references, preserve a correct hyperlink for each displayed number. Retain existing reference macros, or use individual `\ref` calls or compatible `\cref{a,b}` calls. Keep the reference type and its first number together, for example `Lemma~\ref{key}`. Do not link external theorem or equation numbers to similarly numbered items in this manuscript. Never invent a reference. Add a bibliography entry only when its bibliographic data and the specific cited statement have been verified in an inspected copy; otherwise add nothing and record the issue.

### P4. Evidence and uncertainty

Every added explanation must follow from the supplied mathematics. Keep a source location for its support in the editorial record when that support is nontrivial. In the manuscript, supply references where readers need them to follow a nonlocal or ambiguous dependency; an internal audit record is not a reason to add a citation to every inference.

Record suspected mathematical errors, unverified external applications, and transcription questions separately, with exact locations and the evidence actually inspected. Retain the affected mathematical content. Preserve substantive author-supplied scientific caveats. Do not add access limitations, audit markers, or editorial uncertainty notices to the paper as substitutes for scientific exposition.
